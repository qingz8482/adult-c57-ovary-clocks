**Supplementary Figure 2 | Component attribution and gene-level interpretation of the locked clocks.** Panels a–d: Version B–PCA. Panel e: HVG–EN.

**a,** Coefficient paths of all 10 PC inputs along the regularization path of the deployed fit (136 profiles, 14 studies; 3,000 genes; α = 1). PC1, PC2, PC5 and PC8, which have nonzero deployed coefficients, are coloured by sign; the other PCs are grey. Red line: deployed λ = 53.4, chosen by inner leave-one-study-out CV with the one-SE rule (Supplementary Fig. 1a). Dashed line: λ at the minimum inner MAE. Top axis: number of nonzero PC coefficients at that λ. Coefficients are in weeks per PC score unit. No age calibration is applied.

**b,** Conditional leave-one-active-PC-out diagnostic.
- The full four-PC baseline and each three-PC ablation were each retuned and refitted with leave-one-study-out outer folds over 136 profiles from 14 studies.
- Within each outer training set, α and λ were selected by inner leave-one-study-out CV with the same grid, study-balanced weights and one-SE rule as the locked model.
- Points show the paired change in mean absolute error, MAE without the PC minus MAE of the full refit. Bars are 95% percentile intervals from 5,000 paired sample bootstrap resamples.
- The deployed gene selection, scaling and PCA basis were held fixed. This is therefore a conditional component diagnostic, not nested validation.
- Sample-bootstrap intervals ignore study dependence. Equal-study means with study-bootstrap intervals are given in the source data.

**c,** Exact per-PC contributions (coefficient × PC score) for all 136 training profiles.
- Profiles are ordered by chronological age and split into 20 equal-count bins of 6–7 profiles. Each bin is labelled by its age range in weeks, so the bins are not evenly spaced in age.
- Each cell is the median contribution in the bin. Red increases predicted age; blue decreases it.
- Top tracks: bin-median chronological and predicted age, on the same scale in weeks. Predictions span a narrower range than chronological age (median 17–43 vs 7–78 weeks), which is regression toward the training mean (Supplementary Fig. 1j).
- For each profile, the intercept plus the four contributions reproduces the deployed prediction (maximum discrepancy 7.1 × 10⁻¹⁵ weeks). Medians of contributions need not sum to the median prediction.

**d,** Back-projected gene slopes in weeks per unit of ln(1 + CPM): loading × PC coefficient ÷ gene SD.
- The Integrated column sums the four active-PC slopes.
- Rows are the deduplicated union of the strongest positive and negative per-PC and integrated weights, capped at 40.
- Gene centring is absorbed into the intercept.
- The reconstruction reproduces the PC-space predictor within 9.2 × 10⁻¹⁴ weeks.

**e,** Pathway over-representation among the 79 nonzero genes of the locked HVG–EN clock. Genes were split by coefficient sign: 42 genes toward younger predicted age (top) and 37 toward older (bottom).
- **Test.** One-sided hypergeometric ORA against a universe of the 2,952 Entrez-mapped HVG candidate genes, testing GO (BP, CC, MF), KEGG, Reactome and Hallmark sets.
- **Shown.** All terms with Benjamini–Hochberg q < 0.05, grouped by database and ordered by q. The x-axis is −log₁₀ q on a shared scale; the dashed line marks q = 0.05.
- **Labels.** Genes in the term / mapped genes in the list.
- **Interpretation.** Enrichment is conditional on the HVG candidate universe and is not evidence of causal ageing effects.

All Version B–PCA analyses (a–d) use the 136 model-development profiles. The HVG–EN clock (e) was trained on 110 profiles from 9 multi-age studies.
